Uforiya

Every track. Every version. Every collaborator. Nothing lost.

Open beta20+ users
uforiya.com ↗

A collaboration workspace for unreleased music. Every version of a song, its stems and everyone working on it live in one place, instead of a folder full of files named final_v3_REALFINAL.

Producers, artists, songwriters, engineers and studios work on the same track from anywhere, with feedback pinned to the exact second it's about. I'm growing it into a creative ecosystem: software that helps musicians from the first idea to the final master.

I built it foundation first, and wrote an article on the technical decisions behind it. Give it a read.

Try the beta Read: Foundation First

Version history

Features, and the problems they fix
v1Every version keptfile chaos

The fix: New versions stack on the same track instead of piling up as final_v3_REALFINAL. Nothing gets overwritten, and the owner picks which one is current.

v2Feedback on the secondvague notes

The fix: Comments pin to the exact moment in the track, so "the part near the middle" becomes 1:42.

v3Roles and creditslost credits

The fix: Invite people by username or email, give each one a role, and whoever made something keeps the credit.

v4Stems on every versionscattered files

The fix: Stems live with the version they belong to, and only the people working on the track can see them.

v5Share linksfiles in DMs

The fix: One link for a track or a whole project. Private music stays private.

v6Free audio toolsquick fixes

The fix: Trim, fade, convert, compress, change pitch or tempo, and normalize right in the browser, no DAW needed. Four of them work without an account.

v7Private sessionssession chaos

The fix: A camp or session has a lot of people making music at once, and files end up everywhere. A private session gives everyone one place to upload, credits stay with whoever made each track, and the owner covers the storage.

Producers buy kits constantly, and the same samples end up in five folders under five different names. Sample Organizer finds every exact copy and clears them in one click, while one copy of each sample always stays. It runs fully on your computer and never uploads anything.

    How it stays safe

    Deleting the wrong sample from someone's library is the one mistake this app can't make, so safety lives in the engine, not just the screen.

    • Exact byte-for-byte matching, no guessing
    • One copy of every group always survives
    • Play button on every copy before you decide
    • Removals go to quarantine, one click puts them back
    • Permanent delete is a separate, deliberate step

    How it's built

    An Electron desktop app. I also built near-duplicate detection with MFCC audio fingerprints, then turned it off: it flagged samples that sounded alike but weren't the same. Exact matches only means it never removes something you wanted.
